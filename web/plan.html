How the planner uses this

  • Balance first. To raise cash it sells what is overweight against your targets, and protects what is underweight, so you finish as close to your intended mix as possible. Targets that don't add to 100% are scaled; holdings with no target share what's left. With no targets at all, your current mix is the target and a sale is spread in proportion.
  • Judged against its purpose. A holding's return is compared with the expected return you've given it (role defaults: growth 8%, balanced 6.5%, defensive 4%, short-term 3%), and that verdict is weighted by how much of its intended horizon has passed. A 10-year growth holding that's two years in counts at 20%, so a slow patch isn't punished; a short-term holding that has had its full horizon counts in full.
  • Only a nudge. The verdict shifts a holding's target by at most 10% of itself (1% for each point ahead or behind), so performance can tilt a close call but never override diversification. Holdings with no purpose set are never judged on performance.
  • Then tax. Within each holding it sells parcels held over 12 months first, lowest tax first. It only uses a younger parcel if avoiding it would leave you more than 2.5 points further from your target mix.
